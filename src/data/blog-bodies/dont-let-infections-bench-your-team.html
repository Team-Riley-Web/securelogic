What happens when a key player is sidelined, not by injury, but by an infection picked up from the locker room floor?

In high-contact sports, skin infections spread fast. Facilities that host daily practices and games can quickly become hotspots for bacteria and fungi if disinfection is inconsistent or incomplete. Athletes and staff need to be aware of the risks and, more importantly, know how to mitigate them.

Infections That Threaten More Than the Game

Close physical contact, shared equipment, and humid environments create ideal conditions for the spread of infections. According to a 2020 study published in Advances in Clinical and Experimental Medicine, skin infections are responsible for up to 20% of lost training and competition time in contact sports. In the U.S., the incidence among wrestlers alone ranges from 8.5% to 20.9%. These infections are second only to upper respiratory illnesses as the most frequent cause of medical consultations for high school and college wrestlers.

The most common culprits include:

  • MRSA (Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus aureus): A drug-resistant bacteria that spreads easily through direct contact or shared surfaces. It can survive for days on locker room benches, gear, and towels.
  • Ringworm (Tinea): A fungal infection that thrives in damp, warm areas like mats, showers, and gear bags.
  • Staph Infections: These can start from a simple cut and quickly escalate if not caught early. Even non-resistant strains pose a serious threat when combined with the physical demands of contact sports.

These aren’t rare issues. They’re recurring problems that can impact team performance, athlete health, and a facility’s reputation.

The Cost of Poor Disinfection

One infection can quickly lead to a team-wide outbreak. That means missed practices, delayed competitions, and long recovery periods. For facility managers and coaches, the stakes are high. A clean facility is no longer a luxury; it’s a necessity. It’s a non-negotiable part of running a well-managed, successful program.
