The Risks in Confined Spaces

Marine vessels present unique challenges in terms of infection control. Whether it’s a cargo ship, cruise liner, or offshore platform, the risk of illness increases due to close quarters, shared spaces, and limited access to medical care.

Illnesses at sea can start with one individual, yet can impact many. A single infection can disrupt operations, delay travel, or trigger costly quarantines. Many outbreaks can be traced back to one common problem: ineffective surface disinfection.

Pathogens like norovirus, COVID-19, and multidrug-resistant bacteria have caused serious issues in vessels. Once present, they can move quickly through the crew or passenger population.

High-Risk Areas Onboard

Specific locations on a vessel carry a higher risk of spreading germs. These include:

  • Crew cabins and bunk areas
  • Kitchens, break rooms, and mess halls
  • Restrooms and communal showers
  • Navigation rooms and equipment stations
  • Laundry and supply rooms
  • Passenger cabins and gathering spaces on cruise ships

Salt air, high humidity, and limited ventilation all contribute to making it easier for pathogens to survive. Routine cleaning often isn’t enough to eliminate them.
