What’s Hiding on High-Touch Surfaces?

From elevator buttons and door handles to checkout counters and conference tables, certain areas get touched hundreds, sometimes thousands of times a day. And that makes them hotspots for microbial contamination.

Surfaces like these often harbor:

  • Bacteria like E. coli, Staphylococcus aureus (including MRSA), and Salmonella
  • Viruses such as norovirus, influenza, RSV, and even SARS-CoV-2 (the virus that causes COVID-19)
  • Fungi and molds that can trigger allergies and respiratory irritation
  • Biofilms, which are colonies of microorganisms that stick to surfaces and are more resistant to routine cleaning
  • Residues from food, beverages, or bodily fluids that can feed bacteria and lead to foul odors

Even something as simple as a shared touchscreen or a credit card machine can carry pathogens for hours, or longer if left uncleaned. According to studies, certain viruses can survive on surfaces for up to 72 hours, depending on the material and environmental conditions. This silent microbial buildup can lead to the spread of illness among staff and customers alike. Routine disinfection of high-touch areas isn’t just good practice, it’s necessary. It helps reduce the risk of outbreaks, protect vulnerable populations, and keep environments healthier overall.

Harsh Chemicals Don’t Always Equal Better Clean

People don’t always realize that not all disinfectants are suitable for repeated, daily use. Many traditional cleaning products rely on harsh chemicals that can irritate skin, trigger respiratory issues, or leave behind strong odors. That’s not ideal for employees who handle them daily, or for customers walking into a freshly sprayed room.

That’s why botanical disinfectants are gaining momentum. Products made with ingredients like thyme oil provide powerful antimicrobial protection without the health trade-offs. They’re practical, natural, and easier on people, pets, and the environment.
